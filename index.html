The Crisis

Three forces are draining a city that was once defined by its lakes.

i. Industrial

Industrial Pollution

Untreated effluent from factories and tanneries flows directly into lakes. The IISc study of 105 lakes in 2017 found only four in good condition, the rest carrying chemical loads that fish and humans cannot survive.

ii. Urban

Urban Encroachment

Lakes are filled in for housing, malls, and roads. Bangalore had over 1,000 lakes a century ago. By the 1960s, only 280. Today, fewer than 80 survive, a loss of more than 90% of the city's water heritage.

iii. Subterranean

Groundwater Depletion

The city extracts 207 billion litres of groundwater every year while nature recharges only 81 billion. The arithmetic is unforgiving: a deficit hollowing out the ground beneath Bangalore.

About PureStream

A student-led awareness project.

PureStream is a student-led awareness website created as a school project at Candor International School, Bangalore. Our goal is simple: to research, understand, and communicate the growing water contamination crisis in our city, and to make that information accessible to everyone.

For this project we researched water contamination issues specific to Bangalore, gathered statistics from public institutions including the Indian Institute of Science, the Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board, and the Karnataka State Pollution Control Board, and collected expert opinions on the global water crisis.

This website is the result of that work.

How You Can Help

We are students, not an organisation. But awareness starts with individuals.

  1. Learn

    Understand the scale of the problem. Read the IISc study, look at the BWSSB data, follow local journalism on Bangalore's lakes.

  2. Share

    Send this site to friends, family, classmates, teachers. Post the statistics. Awareness scales when it is repeated.

  3. Reduce

    Cut water waste at home. Fix leaks. Rainwater-harvest where you can. Every household decision compounds across a city of fourteen million.

  4. Support

    Back local environmental groups already doing the work: lake restoration trusts, citizen science networks, neighbourhood clean-up drives.
